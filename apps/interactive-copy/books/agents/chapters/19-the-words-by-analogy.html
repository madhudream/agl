In this appendix, we will learn nothing new and understand everything better. Every term the book uses is here, with a one-line meaning and a picture to hang it on. Read it once straight through before Part II if the vocabulary is new to you, and come back to it whenever a word stops you. The pictures are the newsroom's where the newsroom has one, and everyday ones where it does not.

The analogy: the stylebook

Every newsroom keeps a stylebook: the agreed meaning of every word the paper uses, so that two desks never mean different things by "source" or "lede". This appendix is the stylebook for the book. When a chapter uses a word, this is what it means, and this is the picture behind it.

Common questions

Why a newsroom for everything?

One analogy, used consistently, is worth more than ten clever ones: once the reporter, the desk, the editor and the archive are fixed in your mind, every new term has a place to go. Where the newsroom has no picture, we used an everyday one and said so.

Are these definitions exact?

They are exact enough to read the book and the code. A token is not always four characters; a thread is more subtle than "several things at once". The chapters give the precise version where it matters; the picture is for remembering.

Which ten terms matter most?

Token, prompt, evaluator, rubric, state, wave, router, back edge, trace and exam. Know those and the rest follow.

Where are the words defined in code?

Tokens and cost in agl/llm.py; the loop's words in agl/loop.py; tools in agl/tools.py; the graph's words in agl/graph.py; plugins in agl/plugins.py; memory in agl/memory.py and agl/memlib.py; the exam in agl/eval.py.

Carry this

  • Tokens are column inches; the paper pays for every one, and the brief gets longer every round.
  • The loop is a reporter; the evaluator is the editor; the graph is the desk's plan; the state is the whiteboard.
  • A deterministic check is the junior's checklist; a judge is the senior reader; the rubric is the grading sheet.
  • Memory is the archive: pull the clippings before, file the story after, one drawer per client.
  • The exam is the daily edition; the improvement loop is the night desk; scope, gate and log keep it honest.

Check yourself

1. In one picture each: token, wave, back edge.

Answer

A token is a column inch the paper pays for; a wave is the morning meeting sending several reporters out at once; a back edge is the return path when legal bounces a piece.

2. What is the difference between a deterministic check and a judge, in newsroom terms?

Answer

The check is the junior's byline-and-word-count checklist, free and the same every time; the judge is the senior reader with a grading sheet, paid per read, and only shown copy that passed the checklist.

3. Why does a cache have to miss when one word of the question changes?

Answer

Because a cache is honest only for an identical question; serving yesterday's clipping for a different story would be a wrong answer dressed as a fast one.
